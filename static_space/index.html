A rice-straw boiler you can turn the dials on

A 3D cutaway of the boiler, driven live by a heat-loss model built from its own design workbook. Drag to turn it, scroll to zoom. The gas path, its temperatures and the fuel analysis come from the workbook. At the design point the boiler is 81.3 percent efficient.

1. The boiler

Left to right: furnace, settling chamber, convection bank (127 tubes), economizer (water tubes in blue), stack. The glowing dots are flue gas: colour = temperature, speed and number = how much gas the air setting produces. Furnace, settling chamber and bank temperatures are the workbook’s iterated values and stay fixed; the stack temperature and the air setting are yours.





2. Run a 12-hour shift

The fuel gets wetter and drier. A policy sets the excess air every 30 minutes and the 3D boiler follows. The score is the gap to the best possible setting.






Assumption, not measurement: the workbook has no penalty for too little air, so a stylised rule adds one: below a minimum air (35 % plus 0.15 % per kg of extra fuel water per tonne) the efficiency drops 0.25 points for every point of air missing. Under that rule the best setting at each step is exactly that minimum. Replace it with a plant curve before drawing conclusions.

  • The model reproduces the workbook’s flue gas mass (6,958 kg per tonne of fuel) and its oxygen share (7.77 %).
  • It is a design calculation, not plant data. No measured plant data is used, and the unburnt and radiation losses are fixed values from the workbook.
  • An earlier machine-learning test found nothing better than a straight line, because the data came from a straight-line formula. That is why the heat-loss model is now the core.
