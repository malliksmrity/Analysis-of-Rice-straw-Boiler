Energy · biomass boiler · modelling

In this boiler model, less extra air means higher efficiency. Machine learning only re-learns the formula.

Efficiency in the model runs from 91.2 to 95.4 percent. It depends on one thing: the share of excess air. Load makes no difference. Random forest, XGBoost and gradient boosting all land at about the same error as the formula that created the data.

The data is generated from a simplified combustion formula based on a boiler design study (RDME 2025). It is not measured plant data.

How it works

  1. For loads of 60 to 100 percent and excess air of 30 to 80 percent, the formula gives air flow, oxygen in the flue gas and efficiency. A little random noise is added. This makes 2,091 rows.
  2. Several models learn to predict efficiency from these rows and are scored on rows they did not see.
  3. Extra checks: compare with a straight-line baseline, test outside the training range, and look for the best excess air.

Limits

  • The data comes from the formula, so a good score shows that the model can copy the formula, not that it describes a real boiler.
  • The formula has no loss for unburnt fuel or carbon monoxide. Real boilers lose efficiency when air is too low, so a real optimum is above the lowest air value. This model can not find it.
  • The earlier README said the best excess air is 40 to 50 percent. The model and the formula both put it at 30 percent, the edge of the tested range, so that text was corrected.
  • The Streamlit app was trained on five hand-entered rows. It was moved to the archive folder.
