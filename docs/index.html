Energy · biomass boiler · final-year engineering project, with Thermax

A rice-straw boiler turns about 81 percent of the fuel heat into steam. The stack and the unburnt fuel decide most of the rest.

Built from the boiler’s own design workbook: fuel analysis, flue gas species, heat capacities, a 200 °C stack and a 70 percent excess-air design point. At that point 11.6 percent of the heat leaves with the flue gas, 5.5 percent is unburnt and 1.6 percent is radiated.

The model reproduces the workbook’s flue gas mass (6,958 kg per tonne of fuel) and its oxygen share (7.77 %). It is a design calculation, not measured plant data.

What machine learning added

An earlier part of the project trained random forest, XGBoost and gradient boosting on 2,091 generated rows. Their error (MAE 0.14 points) was no better than a straight line (0.12), because the labels came from a straight-line formula. Outside the training range the tree models failed (error 0.84 against 0.12). That is why the heat-loss model is now the core of the project. Machine learning could still help later as a fast stand-in for the slow furnace, bank and economizer iteration.

How it works

  1. Burn the fuel on paper: carbon, hydrogen, sulphur and moisture give carbon dioxide, water vapour and sulphur dioxide. Nitrogen and extra oxygen come from the air.
  2. Heat all of those gases from 30 to 200 °C using the heat-capacity polynomials from the workbook.
  3. Efficiency is 100 minus the flue gas loss, the unburnt loss and the radiation loss.

Limits

  • The unburnt (5.5 %) and radiation (1.6 %) losses are fixed values from the workbook. Real boilers lose more unburnt fuel when air is too low, so the best air setting can not be found here. The README shows what an assumed low-air penalty would do and says it is an assumption.
  • The Monte Carlo ranges are my assumptions, not measurements.
  • No measured plant data is used. Comparing with a commissioning or test run is the next step.
  • In the workbook’s furnace sheet, heat generation is 8.32 MW while 2,957 kg/h at 2,609 kcal/kg gives 8.97 MW. This should be checked in the original.
